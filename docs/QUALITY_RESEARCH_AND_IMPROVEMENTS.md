# VoxForge: quality-led narration and picture improvements

## What I reviewed

I inspected the current flagship's actual script, Stage A-D report, Stage E scene plan/audit, narration TTS settings, mastering chain, and x264 render settings. The current master is 1080p24, H.264, 48 kHz AAC, measured at -15.99 LUFS / -1.25 dBTP; its Stage E audit reports 67/67 validated scenes. That is strong technical compliance, but a compliance score cannot establish that prose is engaging or that the synthetic voice sounds human. The script sample also exposes the main editorial risks: fact-dense clauses, oversized sentences, inflated abstractions, and a misleading cross-act narration line (the “Ming Frontier Network” label appears while discussing the Great Wall/Xiongnu frontier). These are qualitative issues a pixel-family classifier cannot catch.

## Research synthesis

- Documentary prose must be written for listening, not silent reading: conversational wording, active constructions, short comprehensible sentences, read-aloud review, and deliberate room for pictures/silence. New Doc Editing, “Ten Tips for Writing Documentary Narration” (accessed 2026-10-27): https://newdocediting.com/ten-tips-for-writing-documentary-narration/.
- A narration-led edit should let the voice set the story rhythm; the picture can then be cut around meaningful clauses and events. This is an editorial practice, not a claim that every cut must align to every word. Research overview: Journalism University, “Mastering Voice-over Narration for Documentary Impact”: https://journalism.university/electronic-media/voice-over-narration-documentary-tips/.
- Loudness is delivery-context dependent. EBU R 128's broadcast programme target is -23 LUFS, while this repository's -16 LUFS profile is intended for web/spoken-word playback. They must not be presented as the same standard. Official EBU R 128 publication: https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128.
- Picture quality depends on shot sequencing, not just resolution: wides establish place/scale; medium/detail shots reveal action or structure; the next cut should change angle/size with a clear purpose. References: Journalism University, “Exploring Shot Sizes” (https://journalism.university/electronic-media/exploring-shot-sizes-close-ups-long-shots/); Adobe, “Camera shots and angles” (https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/production/cinematography/camera-shots-and-angles.html).
- Kokoro's official voice documentation warns that very short utterances and long/rushing text can underperform; punctuation, phrase length, pronunciation, and voice selection need human listening checks, not just loudness normalization. Official voice notes: https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md.

## Changes in this pass

1. Strengthened local-LLM writer instructions: verified facts only, no invented figures/causes/quotes, 1–3 sentences per turn, at most 45 words, mostly 8–20 words per sentence, active/concrete language, jargon explained, less list-cramming and generic praise, and no narration that merely repeats the obvious picture.
2. Raised ordinary 1080p render quality from CRF 24.8 to CRF 21, retaining a slightly more storage-efficient CRF 23 for marathon CPU renders. This prioritizes legible small typography, fine map lines, gradients, and subtle motion in the code-rendered graphics.
3. Replaced the compact-documentary opener's generic “forty-five centuries of human ambition / outer boundary” exposition with a concise question a narrator can deliver naturally.
4. Corrected a specific inspected visual error: the Great Wall simulation previously titled the Qin/Meng Tian frontier a “Ming Frontier Network” and claimed “700 km/h” signal propagation without grounding. The new graphic says “Great Wall Beacon Network: Signals Across the Northern Frontier,” labels itself a historical concept model, and drops the unsupported speed and network-span claims. The scene caption is now dynasty-neutral.
5. Fixed quote-card retrieval after inspecting the rendered frame: it previously chose an Artemis quotation from the chapter title for a generic Great Pyramid question. Quote lookup now keys off the spoken turn, and an unmatched turn is displayed as a VoxForge narration excerpt rather than falsely attributed archival text. The quote-card scene plan and audit family now permit its real monument background. Added a regression test for unrelated-quote leakage.
6. Preserved the existing TTS normalization, 48 kHz mastering, measured -16 LUFS web profile, and -1 dBTP ceiling; no unsupported EQ/compression changes were made without listening evidence.

## Limits and next validation

The local LLM prompt is only used when a local Ollama/GGUF model is available. The deterministic corpus showrunner is the default, so the changed compact opening and direct renderer fixes were also applied independently; prompt changes alone are not counted as the cause of this run's prose improvement. Automatic script and frame-family audits are not substitutes for listening to the complete narration or watching the whole film. I inspected decoded frames of the corrected quote scene and the Meng Tian/Xiongnu simulation; the Great Wall beat now shows tower/beacon-network visualizations, and the quote card displays the exact active narration line rather than the unrelated Artemis quote.

## Follow-up quality pass (requested after prior result was rejected)

A 12-frame decoded review showed that the visuals were too uniformly wireframe-heavy: the same cyan CAD treatment appeared on ordinary cinematic shots as well as technical inserts. The renderer now intentionally separates the lens passes. `lens_variant=0` uses shaded architectural surfaces and restrained warm silhouette accents; a geometry-only warm-neutral grade corrects the blue cast without changing sky or sun pixels. Technical variants retain the LiDAR point cloud, compass/grid, and cyan CAD lines. The approved twilight-orb optics remain unchanged. A frame-level A/B regression test checks that the cinema material pass is warmer while the technical pass stays cool.

The prior alternating male/female co-host delivery made the documentary sound like a panel discussion. Documentary-writing sources recommend a consistent narrator for expository films, and Kokoro's own voice documentation notes that voice choice and chunk size affect quality. This pass converts finished generated turns to one continuous lead voice (Dr. Maya Lin), selects the documented A-grade `af_heart` voice instead of blending three male voices, centers narration in the stereo image, and changes the editorial gate from a two-host balance target to narrator continuity. The working script can still use question/answer-like beats, but they are delivered as one voiceover, not acted as a synthetic conversation.

A second decoded review exposed a planner defect the family classifier could not expose: short clauses were resolved using only the clause and a chapter title containing multiple monuments. The plan could therefore point at a different 3D model from the full spoken turn. Clause resolution now checks the exact clause, then the complete active spoken turn, then the chapter fallback; the renderer carries that context through b-roll selection and the scene plan. Stage E now independently compares planned scene identity against the actual script turn and refuses certification for a conflict. The complete active turn is threaded through all visual styles (map, close-up, reaction, studio insert, quote, split-card and b-roll), not merely recorded in the plan. Paired-site telemetry is filtered by the active monument so Christ the Redeemer's statistics cannot appear under a Taj Mahal beat, or Chichén Itzá's metrics under Machu Picchu. The Machu model's former concentric-cylinder ziggurat has also been replaced with contour terraces and open Inca compounds, and its camera is a wider master. Regression tests lock the Machu Picchu/Chichén case, paired-metric filtering, and audit rejection behavior. This is a meaning-level fix, not a classifier-threshold workaround.

Reviewing the prior render also showed that its frame-family checker gave a misleading pass: the frames were monument-like but the planned identity was wrong. Stage E returned 46/100 once the new subject-identity check was added; this cut is explicitly not certified.

The opening factual hook was rewritten to short, concrete spoken lines (“Greek writers named seven wonders. Six are gone. Khufu's Great Pyramid still rises above Giza…”), replacing a long compound sentence with an unsupported attribution to “human greed.” This follows documentary writing guidance to make each line understandable once, vary sentence length, and use image/silence for part of the storytelling.

## Rendered result

The flagship was re-rendered after the source improvements. Final pipeline report: **Studio Certified, composite 99.7/100**; Stage B **99.5**, **-16.00 LUFS**, **-1.25 dBTP**, **4.30 LU LRA**, Kokoro narration; 4.76 minutes, 579 words. After the quote-card correction, the final-video Stage E audit reports **100/100, 68/68 scenes validated, zero mismatches, zero review-queue scenes, 0.000 s A/V drift, captions 6/6**. The inspected quote-card excerpt was manually overlaid in the final MP4 to correct the misleading earlier quote. The pipeline's quality-first render uses CRF 21; the corrected final master was re-encoded with x264 slow/CRF 23 to stay below GitHub's 100 MB file limit while preserving 1080p24. Final file is 87 MB. Focused regression tests: 17 passed before the final audit; 8 quote/audit regressions passed afterward.

The full narration has not been perceptually listened to end-to-end in this environment. Thus the measured audio compliance and script review are evidence, but not a claim that the synthetic voice is indistinguishable from a human narrator. The next improvements should be based on a human listen-through, especially names, emotional phrasing, and music-under-speech balance.
